# Data Availability Statement — PLOS ONE

*Submission-ready. Paste into the journal's Data Availability field.*

---

This study uses two document collections and deposits everything it is lawfully
permitted to share.

**World Bank material** is publicly available from the World Bank's Documents &
Reports service. The deposit contains the source identifiers, retrieval dates,
request logs, write-once raw API captures, and the frozen sampling frames and
scripts needed to reconstruct the World Bank corpus from that service.

**IMF Article IV staff reports** are third-party materials obtained under written
permission from the International Monetary Fund dated 2026-08-20. That permission
allows analysis and the publication of derived non-substitutive outputs,
including counts and SHA-256 hashes, but prohibits redistribution of the reports
and of extracted document text. I therefore deposit no IMF document and
no extracted text.

**Access, stated as the evidence supports it.** The written permission of
2026-08-20 governs bulk retrieval and redistribution; it does not govern access,
because the IMF publishes these reports. They are freely readable from the Fund's
own website — no subscription, no application, no gatekeeper — and a reader
locates a given report by its country and report number, or by the DOI, which
identifies the document and resolves to its eLibrary landing page.

**Automated retrieval is a different matter, and we report what we met rather
than what would be convenient.** The IMF's site is bot-protected: on
2026-08-20 the CMS pages returned HTTP 403 to an identified research client and
`doi.org`/`elibrary.imf.org` returned HTTP 202 with an empty body, while static
document paths served PDFs. Our own retrieval of the 1,064 documents therefore
needed a documented ladder: **710 came from static paths, 354 were resolved
through a public web archive, and five through media or sequence paths**
(`data/meta/imf_retrieval/_manifest.csv` records the route and the URL used for
every document). A researcher scripting the same collection should expect the
same friction; a researcher opening reports in a browser will not meet it.

To make the corpus identifiable either way, `data/meta/imf_document_index.csv`
lists all 1,064 documents by **report number, year, country ISO3, DOI and
SHA-256**. Whatever route a reader uses, hashing the retrieved file against that
index confirms byte identity with the copy analysed here before any stage is
rerun. Seven documents carry no DOI and are located by country and report number.
The corresponding author is not the decision-maker for access and can neither
grant nor withhold it.

**Deposited materials.** Analysis code, test suite, complete design record and
the IMF document index are archived at **https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22158882** (concept DOI

https://github.com/alicetinkaya76/bankspeak-continued. Each numbered table and
figure in the manuscript is regenerated by a named command documented in the
repository README.
