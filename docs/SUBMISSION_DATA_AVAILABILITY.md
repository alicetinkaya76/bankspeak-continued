# Data Availability Statement — PLOS ONE

*Submission-ready. Paste into the journal's Data Availability field.*

---

This study uses two document collections and deposits everything it is lawfully
permitted to share.

**World Bank material** is publicly available from the World Bank's Documents &
Reports service. The deposit contains the source identifiers, retrieval dates,
request logs, write-once raw API captures, and the frozen sampling frames and
scripts needed to reconstruct the World Bank corpus from that service.

**IMF Article IV staff reports** are third-party materials obtained under written
permission from the International Monetary Fund dated 2026-08-20. That permission
allows analysis and the publication of derived non-substitutive outputs,
including counts and SHA-256 hashes, but prohibits redistribution of the reports
and of extracted document text. The authors therefore deposit no IMF document and
no extracted text.

**Access does not run through the authors.** The IMF publishes these reports
itself, so any researcher can obtain the same source material directly from the
publisher: each report is retrievable from the IMF's own publication service by
its country report number, and by DOI through the IMF eLibrary
(https://elibrary.imf.org). To make that route usable, the deposit includes
`data/meta/imf_document_index.csv`, which lists **all 1,064 documents by report
number, year, country ISO3, DOI and SHA-256**. A reader resolves the DOI,
downloads the report from the IMF, hashes the file, and confirms byte identity
with the copy analysed here before rerunning any stage. Seven documents carry no
DOI and are reached by report number at the same service. The corresponding
author is not the decision-maker for access and can neither grant nor withhold
it.

**Deposited materials.** Analysis code, test suite and complete design record:
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22152945 (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.22152944),
archived from https://github.com/alicetinkaya76/bankspeak-continued. Evidence
deposit: document identifiers and source metadata; retrieval, exclusion and
quality-control logs; SHA-256 hashes for every restricted source file; derived
document- and year-level counts with token denominators; feature-family counts;
annual panel cells; validation batteries; the family verdict; decision and
deviation records; and the generated tables and figures. Each numbered table and
figure in the manuscript is regenerated by a named command documented in the
repository README.

**Preregistration.** Stage-A: https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/5C9J8. Stage-B
statistical analysis plan, externally timestamped 2026-08-25T15:01:07Z before any
reported outcome was computed: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22098259 (sha256
4aa122797f2db6ddd3e1dae5cb425958b231f02438f242bde174b25b20af2677).

## Note for the author, not for submission

The hash manifest establishes **identity, not availability**, which is why the
document index and the publisher route above matter more than the hashes do. If
PLOS's data-availability check nonetheless asks for a named contact rather than a
publication service, the IMF's Communications Department is the correct
addressee; that is a fallback, not the primary claim, and the primary claim is
true: these documents are published and anyone can fetch them.
