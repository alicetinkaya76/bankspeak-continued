## Rule 1 — only published counts, and the test is "nothing can still change"

> **18. Soru: Yayımlanmış makale nedir?**
>
> Cevap: Yayımlanmış makaleden maksat, doçentlik şartları içerisinde yer verilen
> tanımdan da anlaşılacağı üzere **başvuru tarihi itibariyle yayım için bütün
> süreçleri tamamlanmış, yayıncının yayımlama ve yayımda bulundurma dışında
> üzerinde herhangi bir tasarrufta bulunamayacağı** dergideki özgün çalışmalardır.
> Buna göre başvuru tarihinden önce elektronik ortamda erişilebilir olsa dahi ön
> basım, ön görünüm, çevrimiçi erken görünüm, "online published" ya da "early
> access", "early view", "available online", "upcoming issues", "in progress",
> "published (early access)", "gelecek sayılar" vb. ibareler içeren, **değişiklik
> olma ihtimali bulunan** çalışmalar yayımlanmış makale olarak değerlendirilemez.

And Q17: a DOI is **not** sufficient on its own — the publication processes must
also be complete.

**The operative test is not the label, it is finality**: all processes complete,
and the publisher holds no discretion beyond publishing and keeping it published.
The excluded list is a list of *provisional* states — "değişiklik olma ihtimali
bulunan", works that might still change.

### What this means for continuous-publication journals — and where it stops being verified

Journals like PLOS ONE and the Nature Portfolio open-access titles do not use an
early-access limbo. On acceptance the article is issued in final form with a
volume, an article number, a DOI and a licence, and nothing further changes. On
the operative test above, such an article is published.

**But ÜAK does not name this publication shape anywhere I could find**, and the
same gap covers an article with final pagination whose *issue* is dated after the
application. The reading above is mine from the operative clause; it is not a
quoted rule. If the venue decision comes to rest on it, get it in writing from
ÜAK's doçentlik unit or the university's personnel directorate before submitting.

**The practical consequence either way:** a traditional issue-based journal that
parks accepted papers in "early access" for months is dangerous for this
timetable regardless of how fast its *decision* is. Submission-to-decision is the
wrong metric. Submission-to-**final-form** is the metric.

## Rule 2 — the points, and why single authorship matters here

Tablo 11 (Sosyal, Beşeri ve İdari Bilimler), madde 1, international article not
drawn from the candidate's own theses:

| index | points |
|---|---|
| SCIE or SSCI — Q1 | **30** |
| SCIE or SSCI — Q2 | **20** |
| SCIE or SSCI — Q3 | **15** |
| SCIE or SSCI — Q4 | 10 |
| AHCI | **20** |
| ESCI or Scopus | 10 |
| other international indexes | 5 |

Binding: at least 10 points from these categories after the doctorate.

> "Tek yazarlı yayınlarda yazar tam puan alır. Çok yazarlı yayınlarda puan
> yazarlar arasında eşit olarak bölünür."

This paper is **single-authored**, so it takes the full score of whatever venue
publishes it. That is worth stating because it changes the arithmetic: a
two-author paper in the same journal would be worth half.

Separately, madde 2 requires national articles in TR Dizin journals, and
Scopus/ESCI does **not** substitute for that requirement. Overall: 100 points
minimum, at least 90 after the doctorate.

## Rule 3 — the predatory rule is about Q4, and it clears the way to pay an APC

> **Yağmacı/şaibeli sayılmayan dergiler:**
>
> WEB of Science'de **Q1, Q2, Q3** sınıfında yer alan dergiler (Editöryal ve/veya
> basım süreçlerinde **ücret talep eden veya etmeyen**), WEB of Science'de Q4
> sınıfında yer alıp editöryal ve/veya basım sürecinde ücret talep etmeyen
> dergiler, [ve] WEB of Science'de Q4 sınıfında yer alıp … ücret talep etmekle
> birlikte sadece ilgili bilim alanı mensuplarının üye olabildiği bir branş
> derneğinin, üniversitenin, enstitünün veya bilimsel bir kurumun yayın organı
> olan ve 2010 yılı öncesinden itibaren basılmakta/yayınlanmakta olup abone usulü
> olarak çalışan ulusal/uluslararası dergiler
>
> **Yağmacı/Şaibeli dergiler: Yukarıdaki kriterlere uymayan Q4 dergiler**

Two consequences, and both are useful:

1. **A Q1–Q3 Web of Science journal is explicitly safe whether or not it charges
   an article processing charge.** The parenthesis says so in as many words. So
   paying HSSC's or PLOS ONE's APC carries no predatory-rule exposure *provided
   the journal sits in Q1–Q3*, which is a fact to verify per journal and per
   category, not to assume.
2. **The predatory category is literally "non-conforming Q4 journals" and nothing
   else.** A journal with no Web of Science quartile at all — a Scopus-only venue
   — is not addressed by this text in either direction. That gap is why a
   fee-charging Scopus-only journal is an unquantified risk, and it is moot now
   that the target is SCIE/SSCI.

Articles in journals judged predatory may appear on the CV but "beyannamede
kullanılamaz".

Also verified, Q23: index status is judged **as of the article's publication
date**, and the candidate is not held responsible for a later de-indexing or
quartile change. So a journal's quartile at submission time is what to check.

## What follows for this paper

- The venue must be **SCIE or SSCI** (AHCI is 20 points and acceptable but scores
  below an SSCI Q1), and ideally **Q1 or Q2** — which also puts it inside the
  safe zone of the predatory rule regardless of fees.
- The venue must reach **final published form**, not merely acceptance, before the
  application. That rules out anything with a long early-access queue no matter
  how quickly it decides.
- Single authorship means the full score, so there is no dilution argument for
  adding co-authors to reach a better venue.
