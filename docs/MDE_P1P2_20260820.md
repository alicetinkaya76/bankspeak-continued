# P1/P2 power: the confirmatory family cannot reach the preregistered standard

Date: 2026-08-20. Status: **pilot-precision result, full curve running.** Decided
and executed under Ali's instruction to make the calls with a publishable SCI
paper as the criterion. This is the most consequential finding of the session
and it is reported before the analysis is run, which is the only time such a
finding is worth anything.

## Why this was run at all

`docs/SAP_READINESS_20260820.md` flagged the P1/P2 MDE as the largest open item
before the SAP freeze. Strictly, G4 (MDE₈₀ ≤ 0.60) is a **P0 candidate-selection
gate**, and P1/P2 is the preregistered default family that becomes primary when
no candidate passes — so no gate formally obliges a P1/P2 power run.

It was run anyway, for a publication reason that outranks the formal one: a
reviewer at GIQ or IP&M will ask whether the study could have detected the
effect it claims or fails to find. A power analysis produced *after* seeing
results is worthless and reviewers know it. §11.5 also requires "support and MDE
diagnostics complete" for the Stage-B gate, and the family that survived is
{P1, P2}.

**No IMF text was touched.** Inputs are the Stage-B frames (metadata), the
sealed Stage-A WB features, and the preregistration's own frozen hooks. This
does not compound the D1 ordering deviation.

result:

- `mde_sim.py`'s default `--base-rate` is `6e-5`, **more than twice** the

- σ_δ computed on cells aggregated to YEAR level returns 0.0000. That is an

  year (as the per-cell seed itself shows). At the correct cell level α̂ =
  0.1082, Pearson χ²/df = 4.95, σ_δ = 0.3205. Substantial overdispersion, not
  Poisson.

## Result — FULL PRECISION, COMPLETE COMPANION GRID

1,000 replicates, B = 9,999 inner nested PASS-P, θ grid by 0.05, calibration
hash bound. All three companion settings the preregistration specifies
(§8: the second panel's effect ∈ {0, θ/2, θ}). Family power = P(≥1 Holm
rejection).

| θ (log points) | companion=zero | companion=half | companion=full |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.00 | 0.039 | 0.039 | 0.039 |
| 0.30 | 0.069 | 0.078 | 0.091 |
| **0.60** — the threshold G4 itself sets | **0.159** | **0.158** | **0.216** |
| 0.90 | 0.315 | 0.314 | 0.393 |
| 1.20 | 0.483 | 0.485 | 0.569 |

**MDE₈₀ = unreachable under every companion setting.** 80% family power is not
attained anywhere on the preregistered grid.

The number to quote is θ = 0.60. That is the value the design chose as its own
branch-selection standard (G4: MDE₈₀ ≤ 0.60), and the confirmatory family
attains **0.159 to 0.216** power there where 0.80 was required — short by a
factor of four to five. Even at θ = 1.20, exp(1.2) ≈ 3.3× the baseline rate,
the most favourable setting reaches 0.569.

Type-I is sound and identical across all three (0.039 against α = 0.05), and
`power_p2` sits at ≈ 0.048 throughout the companion=zero run, exactly as it must
when the companion effect is zero. The engine is behaving; the design is
underpowered.

**MDE₈₀ = unreachable. 80% family power is not attained anywhere on the

Type-I behaviour is sound: family rejection at θ = 0 is 0.03, consistent with
α = 0.05. The engine is not broken; the design is underpowered.

## What drives it, and what does not

**σ_δ is the whole story.** At σ_δ = 0 the same setup gives MDE₈₀ = 0.65. At the
design's own frozen σ_δ = 0.32 it is unreachable.

**Sampling more documents does not help.** Tripling every panel's documents
(cap 40 → ~120/year) moves power at θ = 1.2 from 0.48 to 0.53. The binding
constraint is a YEAR-level differential shock; adding documents within a year
averages Poisson noise, not δ_t. This was measured, not assumed, and it is a
publishable methodological point in its own right: for institution-level
differential designs on rare lexical markers, **document count is not the lever**.

The lever the design does not have is post-period years. There are three
(2023–2025), and §11.4 excludes 2026 from the confirmatory frame.

## Consequences — the reason this matters for publication

1. **A null H-DIFF would be uninformative and must not be reported as evidence
   of no effect.** With this power, "no differential change" cannot be
   distinguished from "no ability to see one." Reporting it as a finding is
   exactly the error a competent reviewer computes in ten minutes.
2. **A significant H-DIFF would need heavy caveating.** At ~50% power for an
   enormous effect, any rejection carries a severe winner's-curse inflation; the
   point estimate would be a poor guide to magnitude.
3. **This belongs in the SAP, before the analysis runs.** Preregistering an
   honest "we could not reach 80% power for any effect in the grid" converts a
   fatal post-hoc discovery into a stated limitation.

## Recommendation

Adopt the framing already argued on other grounds, now with a measured basis:
**RQ1 (the 1946→2026 diachronic extension) is the spine; RQ2 is a preregistered,
explicitly power-bounded probe** reported with this curve, as an interval and a
bound rather than a verdict. That paper is publishable whichever way the
estimate falls. A paper whose headline is a confirmatory H-DIFF claim is not,
because the design cannot deliver one at the standard the design itself set.

Whether this formally triggers the §11.5 fallback (RQ1/measurement paper) is a
preregistration reading and Ali's call. The engineering position is narrower and
firm: **the confirmatory family cannot deliver a confirmatory claim at the
preregistered power standard, and that fact is now on the record before any
outcome was seen.**
