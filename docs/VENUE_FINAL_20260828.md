# Venue: PLOS ONE — and a correction to yesterday's advice

Priority as stated by Ali: **fastest to publication, most likely to be accepted**,
subject to being in Web of Science SCIE or SSCI. Points and prestige secondary.

## The correction: HSSC is out, and my earlier recommendation was wrong

I recommended *Humanities and Social Sciences Communications* targeting an
October 2027 application. That was wrong, and it failed on the exact rule I had
established myself: **ÜAK counts final form, not online publication.**

Springer's Article-in-Press stage applies to every accepted manuscript at its
fully open-access titles. I checked HSSC articles directly:

| article | volume | article no. | published |
|---|---|---|---|
| s41599-026-08416-0 | — | — | 2026-08-28 |
| s41599-026-08842-0 | — | — | 2026-08-27 |
| s41599-026-08811-7 | — | — | 2026-08-27 |
| s41599-025-06403-5 | **13** | **289** | 2025 |
| s41599-025-06277-7 | **12** | **2009** | 2025 |

Articles published *this week* carry no volume, no issue and no article number.
Articles from 2025 do. So assignment happens, with a lag — measured at roughly
3.5–4 months, with the assignment frontier sitting around early May 2026 as of
late August.

That makes HSSC's real submission-to-**countable** time about **15–16 months**:
~12 months to acceptance plus ~4 months of Article-in-Press. From a September
2026 submission that lands in late 2027 to early 2028 — past October 2027, not
just past March.

The same trap eliminates **EPJ Data Science**, **Journal of Big Data**,
**Scientific Reports** (60–88 days before version of record) and **Heliyon**.
Speed of decision is irrelevant when the paper then sits in a state ÜAK excludes
by name.

## The answer: PLOS ONE

### Why it is the most publishable venue for *this* paper

PLOS ONE's seven publication criteria contain **no test of novelty, significance
or impact**. I read them from the journal's own criteria page: original research,
not published elsewhere, high technical standard, conclusions supported by the
data, intelligible English, research ethics, data availability. That is the whole
list.

This is the hinge of the decision. At a conventional journal a reviewer may
reject on "the finding is not interesting", and a preregistered null with power
0.16–0.22 is precisely the paper that invites that sentence. Soundness-only
review does not make the null better; **it removes the reviewer's authority to
reject on that ground.** What is left is "is it technically sound" and "are the
conclusions supported" — and a preregistered null whose power was computed before
any outcome existed passes both, because its conclusion is *we could not detect
an effect, and here is why we would not have.*

Also verified on the journal's own pages:

- **"In keeping with our mission to publish all valid research, we consider
  negative and null results."**
- Scope explicitly names **"related social sciences and humanities"** — no scope
  desk-reject risk.
- Registered Report is a recognised article type.
- **No length limit** — the paper is 9,400 words with 8 tables and 3 figures,
  which is over budget almost everywhere else.
- A third-party-data provision that accommodates the IMF redistribution ban.

### The numbers, from PLOS's own metrics table

| | value |
|---|---|
| Time to publication (median) | **204 days ≈ 6.7 months** |
| Time from acceptance to publication | **10 days** |
| Desk rejection without peer review | 30.8% |
| Acceptance rate | 30.7% |

The most recent cohort published in that table is **Jan–Jun 2023**, so the page is
stale; independent measurement of 2026 articles gives ~186–208 days, which agrees.

**Ten days from acceptance to publication is the number that matters** — it is the
numerical proof that there is no early-access limbo. Volume, issue and article
number are assigned on the publication date.

### Arithmetic

Submit **2026-09-15** + 204 days → **published around 2027-04-07**.
25th percentile ≈ mid-January 2027. 75th percentile ≈ mid-September 2027.

So March 2027 is reachable only in the fast half, and April is the modal outcome.
Since ÜAK runs March **and** October periods, the October 2027 application is
comfortably covered on any but the slowest path.

### What it costs

- **USD 2,477**, mandatory, no subscription route, Türkiye not Research4Life-eligible.
- **20 points, not 30** (SCIE, Q2 in Multidisciplinary Sciences — *secondary-sourced;
  confirm, since Q1 would make it 30*).
- No disciplinary readership in institutional-discourse history. The paper buys a
  countable line and forfeits an audience.
- Rejection risk migrates to **desk screening and the data-availability check** —
  plan against those, not against reviewer taste.

## The alternative worth knowing about: SAGE Open

Rank 2, and genuinely close.

- Verified **Q1** in Social Sciences Interdisciplinary (48/275).
- The strongest soundness-only statement of any venue assessed: accepts articles
  "solely on the basis of the research", with thematic significance explicitly
  disclaimed.
- Scope explicitly includes humanities, with a Research Methods section that
  would suit the unitisation result.
- Explicitly "does not limit content due to page budgets".
- Lower APC than PLOS ONE, and the best data-policy fit in the set.
- **Against it:** ~261 days (≈8.6 months, publishing around 2027-06-03), a 22.1%
  headline acceptance rate, and no observed precedent for a preregistered null at
  the title. Its volume-assignment behaviour I could not verify directly — SAGE
  blocks automated fetches — so that is taken from the research pass, not checked.

**Sustainability (MDPI)** publishes in a measured **43 days** and is ÜAK-clean,
but it disclaims nothing on novelty, so the null has no protection, and an
eighty-year corpus study of World Bank prose would have to enter as
"sustainable-development institutional communication". Speed multiplied by a
coin-flip on a paper you would then have to re-target is not speed. Not
recommended.

## Decision

**PLOS ONE, submitted mid-September 2026.** Expected publication early April 2027;
comfortably inside the October 2027 ÜAK period, and inside March 2027 if the
review draw is kind.

## Before submitting

1. Confirm PLOS ONE's current quartile (20 vs 30 points).
2. Settle the framing question — option A or option B (`FRAMING_OPTION_B_20260828.md`).
3. Run the third-eye review; the kit and brief are ready.
4. Write the data-availability statement to name the IMF restriction, the
   hash-list deposit, and how another researcher would obtain the same access.
   This is where the real rejection risk sits at a soundness-only venue.
