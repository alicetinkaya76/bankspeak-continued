## A7 — why the Stage-B sample differs from the sealed one

Three causes, separated. Reporting them as one number would hide the largest.

**1. Cutoff (PREREG §11.4).** The sealed sample predates the cutoff and carries
**80 rows dated 2026** (40 icr + 40 pad). They leave the confirmatory window
before any other comparison. Sealed 2,818 → 2,738.

**2. Frame drift — negligible.** Over the confirmatory-eligible universe:

| stratum | Stage-A | Stage-B | added | withdrawn | unchanged |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| annual_report | 331 | 331 | 0 | 0 | 331 |
| icr | 8,350 | 8,352 | **2** | 0 | 8,350 |
| pad | 6,387 | 6,387 | 0 | 0 | 6,387 |

The world did not move in fourteen days. Note that those **2 added documents
move 31 sample members**: adding a record to a capped cell reshuffles that
cell's whole 40-document draw. That is the sampler behaving as specified — App.
B.7 guarantees stability against *other strata* changing, not against the cell's
own population changing — but it is worth stating, because it means frame drift
and sample drift are not the same size and never will be.

**3. Sampler change — the dominant cause, and it is proven, not inferred.**

The sealed sample was drawn by `s01`'s **single global RNG** consumed
sequentially across strata and years. PREREG Appendix B.7 instead specifies the
stable per-cell sampler, `seed_cell = SHA256("20260806|{institution}|{genre}|
{year}")`, under which "adding or removing any other stratum cannot change a
cell's draw".

The attribution was verified by reproduction: replaying `s01`'s exact procedure
(`random.Random(20260806)`, `sorted(cfg["strata"])`, years 1946-2026, sort by
`(docdt, id)`, `rng.sample`) over the Stage-A jsonl universe reproduces
`frozen_sampling_v1.csv` **exactly — 2,818 of 2,818, zero on either side**. The
sealed WB sample is demonstrably the global-RNG draw.

Redrawing under the preregistered per-cell sampler therefore changes most of the
sample without anything in the world having changed:

| comparison | left | right | shared | changed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| sealed(cut) vs per-cell(Stage-A) — **sampler alone** | 2,738 | 2,738 | 751 | **1,987 (72.6%)** |
| per-cell(A) vs per-cell(B) — **frame alone** | 2,738 | 2,738 | 2,707 | 31 (1.1%) |
| sealed(cut) vs per-cell(B) — **combined** | 2,738 | 2,738 | **748** | 1,990 |

## The number the prior-inspection ruling needed

**748 of 2,738 documents (27.3%)** in the Stage-B confirmatory WB sample are the
same documents whose outcomes were inspected at Stage-A. Roughly three quarters
of the confirmatory corpus was never looked at.

This materially *reduces* the concern raised in
`docs/RULING_20260820_prior_inspection.md` — and it does so for a reason that
strengthens rather than weakens the design: the Stage-B draw follows the
preregistered sampler, and the Stage-A draw did not.

## Status of the sealed draw — a fact to disclose, not a defect to fix

The Stage-A artifact is sealed and never changes; nothing here edits it. The
benign reading is that `frozen_sampling_v1.csv` was the working sample for the
Stage-A **descriptive** analysis, and PREREG §7.6 always required the
confirmatory frame to be re-captured at the Stage-B snapshot date — which is
what A6 did. Under that reading the global-RNG draw is superseded rather than
violated, and §11.2's Stage-A code precondition (the per-cell sampler must
*exist* and be fixture-tested before the timestamp) was met: `percell_seed.py`
and `s09_frame_sampler.py` were both in the sealed package.

What must not happen is a reader comparing a sealed descriptive number against a
confirmatory one and assuming they describe the same documents. They mostly do
not. That sentence belongs in the paper.
